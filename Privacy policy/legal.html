      8AlphaPack Grey Wolf Optimizer: simulation only, not a mandateNotice

      The AlphaPack Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) is a metaheuristic solver inspired by grey wolf pack hunting behaviour. It searches for candidate portfolio weight vectors that maximise a stated objective — typically a risk-adjusted return such as a Sharpe-like ratio — subject to the constraints you supply. Its purpose is quantitative education and exploration of the optimisation landscape.

      The following apply without limitation:

      • Its output is the arithmetic solution to a mathematical problem under a historical dataset, not a portfolio recommendation. A solution is only as meaningful as the objective function, the constraints, and the data you gave it.
      • The optimiser explicitly suppresses extreme corner-weight concentration. That is a modelling choice designed to demonstrate algorithm behaviour, not a risk profile derived from you.
      • It assumes, among other things, that historical relationships persist. Correlations, volatility regimes, spreads and cross-asset relationships routinely break down, invert, and remain broken for extended periods.
      • It does not model transaction costs, slippage, market impact, taxes, stamp duty, GST, brokerage, custody charges, liquidity constraints, borrow cost, short-sale feasibility, or capacity.
      • It does not perform predictive forecasting. It optimises over observed history; it does not know the future.
      • Overfitting is a material and expected risk. The optimiser will always return a result even when the underlying data is noise, the sample is too short, or the objective is misspecified. A returned solution is not evidence of a genuine edge.
      • Non-determinism, local optima, and sensitivity to initialisation and parameterisation mean repeated runs on similar inputs may differ materially.
      A well-optimised weight vector can still lose money entirely. Optimisation reduces a modelled quantity. It does not protect capital.
      9Backtests, Monte-Carlo simulations and equity curves are hypotheticalNotice

      • They are prepared with the benefit of hindsight and may reflect look-ahead bias — using information that would not have been available at the simulated time.
      • They may reflect survivorship bias — excluding delisted, bankrupt or acquired instruments that a real portfolio would have held and lost.
      • They assume idealised liquidity, frictionless execution, no slippage, no market impact, and no capacity constraint.
      • They generally omit transaction costs, brokerage, taxes, stamp duty, GST, custody charges and borrow costs.
      • They are sensitive to the chosen start date, end date, frequency, rebalancing schedule and data vendor. Small changes can transform results.
      • They assume capital was available and deployed throughout the period, which is rarely true in practice.
      • They do not account for your circumstances: taxes, liabilities, cash needs, risk capacity, or behavioural tendencies.

      No one has been consulted, and no position was actually held. Hypothetical results are not indicative of future performance and carry no guarantee of being achieved in a live account.

      12Risk of total loss of capital across asset classesNotice

      You acknowledge the following risks. This list is not exhaustive and is not a substitute for reading the full documentation of any instrument you trade.

      Market risk
      Prices can and do fall, sometimes sharply and without warning. Market-wide declines can exceed your capacity to absorb. Corrections and bear markets can persist for years.
      Volatility risk
      Rapid, large and adverse price movements are common, including intraday gaps through your stop level, which may execute far below it.
      Liquidity risk
      Small and mid-cap instruments, and some derivatives and digital assets, may not be tradeable at your intended size or price. You may be unable to exit a position at all.
      Model & systemic risk
      Correlations rise sharply in crises. Diversification assumptions that hold in normal periods can fail exactly when you most need them.
      Equity risk
      Company-specific events — fraud, governance failure, debt, dilution, promoter pledging, litigation, regulatory action — can permanently impair value, including to zero.
      Derivative & leverage risk
      Futures and options can expire worthless. Leverage can produce losses exceeding capital. Margin calls can force liquidation at the worst available price. Short positions carry theoretically unlimited loss.
      Options risk
      Options can lose 100% of premium. Complex and multi-leg structures carry defined and undefined risk. Writing uncovered options carries potentially unlimited loss.
      Crypto risk
      Digital assets are speculative, largely unregulated, highly volatile, and vulnerable to exchange failure, insolvency, fraud, hacks, bridge exploits, and permanent total loss.
      Currency risk
      Foreign-denominated assets carry exchange-rate risk that can overwhelm the underlying return.
      Operational & counterparty risk
      Broker failure, exchange outages, order rejection, failed settlement, and payment-system outages can leave you exposed and unable to act.
      Regulatory & policy risk
      Rules on derivatives, taxation, capital gains, crypto, and market access can change retroactively and adversely.
      Psychological risk
      Loss aversion, overconfidence, recency bias, and revenge trading are among the most reliable routes to permanent loss.
