W6 AI hallucination, fabrication, bias and model error

Large Language Models and the algorithmic engines behind the Service can and do produce output that is wrong. This is a fundamental property of the technology, not an occasional defect we are working to eliminate. You must assume that any output you read may be incorrect until you have independently verified it.

Known failure modes

Hallucination
The model generates statements, statistics, citations, company names, regulatory references, dates and technical details that sound authoritative and are entirely fabricated. A fluent sentence is not a true sentence.
Confident error
The most dangerous category. The model states an incorrect figure or conclusion in exactly the same tone and with the same apparent certainty as a correct one. No tone, hedge or formatting cue reliably distinguishes them.
Outdated figures
Model weights carry a knowledge cut-off. Rules, prices, holdings, indices, ratios, tax provisions and company facts may have changed since. The model may describe the world as it was, not as it is.
Misattribution
Data or commentary may be attributed to the wrong instrument, entity, jurisdiction, period or author. A correct fact attached to the wrong subject is a false claim.
Confabulated specifics
Precise-looking numbers — percentages, ratios, CAGRs, Sharpe ratios, weights, tax figures — may be invented. Plausible precision is a symptom of fabrication, not evidence of rigour.
Bias
Training data reflects human bias, recency effects, survivorship in coverage, and over-representation of popular assets.
Mathematical error
Calculations, projections and optimisations can contain arithmetic defects, formula errors, unit mismatches, invalid assumptions, or bad input data. The AI may also misinterpret your question and answer a different one.
Mandatory verification. Every material fact, figure, ratio, rule, tax consequence, price and corporate action that you intend to rely upon must be independently verified against a primary source — the exchange or issuer filing, the SEBI or government publication, your broker's official documentation, or a licensed professional — before you commit capital.

W8 AlphaPack Grey Wolf Optimizer: simulation only, not a mandate

The AlphaPack Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) is a metaheuristic solver inspired by grey wolf pack hunting behaviour. It searches for candidate portfolio weight vectors that maximise a stated objective — typically a risk-adjusted return measure such as a Sharpe-like ratio — subject to the constraints you supply. Its purpose is quantitative education and exploration of the optimisation landscape.

An optimiser output is the mathematically optimal solution to the objective and constraints you gave it. It does not know whether your expected returns are achievable, whether your correlations are accurate or will persist, whether the instruments are liquid or borrowable, or your tax position, liabilities, cash needs, horizon or genuine risk capacity.

A precise, converged, well-formatted allocation vector is a statement about your inputs, not about the market. Two users supplying different inputs will receive materially different allocations; both will be "optimal" for what was asked. Optimiser output is not a recommended portfolio, an advised allocation, an approved mandate, or a model portfolio you should mirror. We do not manage, rebalance or monitor portfolios.

Never treat an AlphaPack allocation as a trade list. It is a teaching instrument.
W9 Backtests, simulations and calculator outputs

Any backtest, equity curve, drawdown chart, Monte-Carlo fan chart, projection, compound-growth estimate or performance attribution displayed on the platform is hypothetical. Hypothetical results have inherent limitations, including:

  • Look-ahead bias — using information that would not have been available at the simulated time.
  • Survivorship bias — excluding delisted, bankrupt or acquired instruments that a real portfolio would have held and lost.
  • Assumptions of idealised liquidity, frictionless execution, no slippage, no market impact, and no capacity constraint.
  • General omission of transaction costs, brokerage, taxes, stamp duty, GST, custody charges and borrow costs.
  • Sensitivity to the chosen start date, end date, frequency, rebalancing schedule and data vendor. Small changes can transform results.
  • An assumption that capital was available and deployed throughout the period, which is rarely true in practice.
  • No accounting for your circumstances: taxes, liabilities, cash needs, risk capacity, or behavioural tendencies.

No one has been consulted, and no position was actually held. Hypothetical results are not indicative of future performance and carry no guarantee of being achieved in a live account. Stock forecasts and price targets carry no guarantee of accuracy, reliability or profitability. Never buy, sell, hold, short or leverage a position on the basis of an AI-generated forecast, price target, allocation or calculator result alone.
